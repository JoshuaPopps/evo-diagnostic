Flash codes — 1998 Sportster XL1200S only

The fuel-injected 1200S is the only Evo-era Harley that stores trouble codes and flashes them out on the check-engine lamp. Carbed Evos have no check-engine lamp and store no codes.

How to read them: jumper wire across pins 1 and 2 of Data Link connector [91A], key to IGNITION, wait about 8 seconds. Rapid flashes (about 3/sec) = intermission, then a 2-second pause; count the flashes for the first digit, 2-second pause, count the second digit. Source: Sportster XLH 1998 Service Manual 99484-98, pp. 7-15 to 7-17.

Tire date

Read the DOT date code off the sidewall — the last four digits of the Tire Identification Number (week + year). Pre-2000 tires use three digits. The full TIN is only on one sidewall; check the inboard side if it is not outside.

Battery & charging — test it in order

From FSM #99482-98 §8. Battery first, then stator, then regulator — a dead battery makes every other reading lie. You need a multimeter, and a load tester for step 2.

Compression test — guided

From FSM #99482-98 §3, pp. 3-11–3-12. Tool: Cylinder Compression Gauge Part No. HD-33223-1, screw-in type adapter. Warm engine, both plugs out, throttle wide open — a cold engine or a closed throttle makes the readings lie.

Idle RPM meter

Hold the phone near the exhaust at idle. The meter reads the exhaust pulse frequency and converts it to RPM. Factory idle: 1000–1050 rpm. Deterministic signal processing — no AI, no guessing.

Listen and compare — YouTube

Links only — the app never downloads YouTube audio. Every label is the uploader’s claim or the thread’s guess, and the note under each link says so. Phone recordings amplify mechanical sounds and dampen the exhaust note, so a bike can sound worse on video than in reality. No usable video exists for piston slap, primary chain, detonation/ping, starter grind, or belt noise — those need your own recordings.

VIN decoder

Decodes year, model family, engine and plant from published VIN structure, with check-digit validation. A Harley VIN does not encode color or options, and no public Harley build-record lookup exists. Model codes below are FSM-verified for 1997–98 Softails; later year codes follow the standard 30-year cycle.

Recalls & service bulletins

Offline bundle from NHTSA (U.S. federal public domain). 12 recall campaigns 1986–1999, 83 Harley bulletins 1995–1999, 61 owner complaints counted by component. No open API can say whether a specific VIN already had a recall fixed — check nhtsa.gov/recalls or a dealer for that.

Torque & specs

Plug reader — read the plug, read the motor

From the 1984–1999 Softail Repair Manual, Ch. 3, p. 112. Pull a plug after a normal ride, look at the firing tip, match it below. The Diagnose tab photo upload gives a rough color read; this card is the reference it reads against.

Wire & breaker — what the factory ran

Evo Softails use circuit breakers, not fuses. Breaker ratings from FSM #99482-98 §8 p. 8-1; wire gauges from the 1997 Main Harness table (App. A Table 2, Part No. 70216-96). The breaker protects the wire — never fit a bigger breaker without bigger wire.

Gear ratio — sprockets in, rpm out

Enter the teeth on your bike. Stock 1997–98 numbers and the book’s own contradiction are below the calculator — count your own teeth, the calculator trusts what you type.

Tap & drill — what bit for which thread

Inch: 1984–1999 Softail Repair Manual, Table 7, p. 32. Metric: Clymer Table 11, pp. 33–34. Both tables read visually from the printed page Nov 25, 2026.

Bolt ID — measure it, name it

Thread series from the same two tables as Tap & drill. A diameter alone never identifies a thread — you need the diameter AND the pitch (metric) or threads-per-inch (SAE).

Oil viscosity — which grade for which weather

FSM #99482-98 §1, p. 1-9. Pick by the lowest temperature expected before your next oil change, not by today’s weather.

Compression ratio — bore, stroke, chamber

Bore, stroke, displacement and ratio are published (Repair Manual Table 1, Ch. 5 p. 179; Sportster Table 1, p. 193). Chamber volume is never published — measure it with a burette and enter it. The derived chamber numbers below are arithmetic on the published ratio, not published figures.

Carb CFM — what the motor needs

CFM = CID × RPM × VE / 3456. This sizes what the engine needs at a given rpm — no published CFM flow rating for the CV40 exists in the library, so none is claimed here. VE is your assumption, not a spec.

Sag setter — set the preload, record the sag

Preload procedure from FSM #99482-98 §2, p. 2-73. No factory sag target is published anywhere in the library — this card records your measurements and never prints a target it cannot cite.

Parts interchange — what fits what

Fitment ranges from the factory parts catalogs and the manuals, plus two magazine interchange tips flagged as magazine. A part with no fitment range in the library does not appear here.

Pre-ride check — T-CLOCS, Evo checks

The checks are Clymer Ch. 3 (Evo) and Sportster Ch. 3, grouped under the MSF T-CLOCS headings. Tick them off before every ride — the list is the same every time, that is the point.
